The Unlabeled Dials

We built a tiny AI that writes a little like Shakespeare. Here's a simple, honest way to see why we can watch it work — but still can't tell you what it's "thinking."

1

2

Numbers nobody wrote a list for

Once the AI has your letters as numbers, it does an enormous amount of math on them. Some of that math produces this — 384 more numbers. These are real, taken from one real moment while our AI was reading the word "ROMEO:". Every square below is a genuine number, not a mockup:

Here are five of those exact numbers, up close:

-0.102?
-0.742?
-0.390?
-0.500?
-0.269?

Now here's the difference from step one: nobody wrote a list for these. There's no page, anywhere, that says "number 6 means the AI is thinking about kings." Not because it's secret or hidden from us — that page was simply never written, by us or by anyone who builds AI like this. It didn't need to exist for the AI to work.

Imagine a soundboard covered in 384 sliders. While a song plays, you can watch every slider move up and down — real, visible motion. But none of them has a label. You can't tell which one is the drums, which is the volume, or whether any of them mean something a person would recognize at all. You're watching real movement. You are not watching an explanation.

So what does "monitoring an AI think" actually mean?

People — including us, earlier in this project — sometimes talk about "watching an AI reason." What we can really watch is step one: the letters going in, and the letters coming out. That part has a real, complete list, so we can honestly say what it means.

Step two is the part people usually mean by "thinking." And for step two, we can watch the numbers appear, in real time, completely honestly — we just showed you 384 real ones. We simply can't tell you what any of them mean, because that list doesn't exist yet. Not for our tiny AI, and not for the giant ones either.
